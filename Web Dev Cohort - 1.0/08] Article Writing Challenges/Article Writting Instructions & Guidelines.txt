## Instructions :

- Word Count
	- Aim for 800-1200 words per article or blog.
	- Adjust based on the topic's depth.

- Stay Authentic
	- Avoid copying content from other sources.
	- Write in your own words to maintain originality.
	- If you reference data, statistics, or concepts, mention where they're from
	- Use a conversational tone, ask rhetorical questions, and provide interesting trivia related to the topic.

- Include Digrams
	- Wherever possible, add diagrams or flowcharts to represent ideas visually.
	- Tools like Eraser, Excalidraw or even hand-drawn sketches (scanned or photographed) can be used.

- Use Examples 
	- Include real-world examples or analogies to make complex topics relatable.
	- For example, compare DNS to a phone book when explaining its function
	
- Structure Your Content
	- Srart with a catchy title that grabs attention.
	- Begin with an introduction that explains the purpose of the article or blog.
	- Use subheadings to divide sections logically.
	- End with a conclusion summarising the key points or giving practical takeaways.

- Write with Clarity
	- Use simple and clear language.
	- Avoid technical jargon unless necessary, and explain terms in layman's language.
	

## Gudielines :

- Introduction
	- Make the first paragraph engaging. For example, start with a question, fact, or relatable example.
	- State what the article is ablout and why it's important to learn.

- Body
	- Use bullet points or numbered lists for clarity when explaining multiple concepts.
	- Create logical connections between sections to maintain flow.

- Conclusion
	- Recap the key learnings.
	- Add a call-to-action, like encouraging readers to try something they learned (e.g., "Check your browser's DNS settings to understand how it works in real-time!").
	
- Writing Style
	- Keep sentences short and easy to read.
	- Use active voice: "The browser sends a request to the server" instead of "A request is sent by the browser."
	- Add personal opinions or reflections where appropriate to humanize the content.
